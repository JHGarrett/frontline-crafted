# Product material estimates

When John asks for a product quote, material cost, or wood-species comparison, first read the product's saved reference:

`src/assets/products/the-<product-name>/reference/lumber-costs.md`

These files are internal planning documents, not website content. Do not import them into the application or display them to customers.

- Use the saved build quantities, species allocations, base choices, plywood requirements, and purchasing notes. Read the whole reference before calculating.
- Compare red oak, maple, white oak, walnut, beech, and cherry when a species comparison is requested.
- Distinguish lumber/material estimates from a finished customer selling price. Do not invent labor, upholstery, hardware, finish, tax, delivery, or margin amounts. State included costs and exclusions.
- For a test or estimate using saved rates, identify them as dated reference prices. For current supplier pricing or a customer-ready quote, verify the linked supplier listings. Never invent missing rates; mark incomplete totals as needing a supplier quote.
- Follow the no-MDF-core plywood preference. Check actual thickness, sheet size, grade, and cut yield; do not silently substitute metric imported panels or 5×5 sheets for requested nominal thicknesses or 4×8 sheets.
- Show the base material total and optional 15% extra lumber separately. Do not add waste twice. Recalculate quantities for custom dimensions rather than assuming the standard build's quantities still apply.
- Confirm missing species allocations rather than treating a two-species design as a single-species build. Belmont, Rowan, Hollis, and Arden require two hardwood species; additional requirements may be recorded later in individual references.
- The Rowan uses both 5 BF of 4/4 and 6 BF of 5/4 together. John's confirmed example uses red oak for 4/4 and white oak for 5/4; these species are an example pairing, not a requirement for every order.
- Preserve customer clarifications in the relevant reference so future sessions can use them. Do not change website product prices based on an internal material estimate unless asked.

References currently exist for Arden, Atlas, Beaumont, Belmont, Bennett, Brazos, Camden, Carrington, Hollis, Langston, Marlowe, and Rowan. If a requested reference is missing, obtain the build requirements before estimating.

## Provisional customer pricing rule

John chose this rule on October 6, 2026: when he asks what to charge, use **selling price = material cost × 2.65** until he supplies tracked build hours or changes the rule. Do not substitute a different pricing method or existing website minimums for this calculation.

Use the material total established for the requested configuration. State what that total includes and what is missing; if only lumber is priced, identify the result as based on lumber only rather than pretending it is complete material cost. Show the optional 15% extra lumber separately; do not silently select or double-count it. Do not invent costs for labor or other missing items. Missing material prices require a supplier quote or a clearly labeled partial calculation. This rule is for requested quote calculations; do not change website prices unless asked.

## Default hardwax finish allowance

John plans to use a hardwax finish such as Rubio Monocoat for most builds and supplied an approximate kit cost of $60–$80. For provisional estimates, budget **one kit at $80 per piece**, unless John supplies a different price, quantity, or finish choice. This is a cost allowance, not a verified can-size or coverage claim. Do not assume overall furniture dimensions establish exact finish volume; that requires the product's coverage rate and all coated surfaces, including undersides, legs, cabinet interiors, and edges.

Include this finish allowance in material cost before applying the provisional selling-price multiplier: **(other priced materials + $80 finish allowance) × 2.65**. If material costs already include finish, do not add it again. For matching sets, clarify whether one kit covers the set rather than silently charging one kit per item when shared usage is specified. Continue to identify unpriced hardware, adhesives, abrasives, upholstery, and other exclusions. Different finish requirements or confirmed coverage needs can change the allowance.
